SIMPLE CRITICAL ZEROS AND DISTINCT ZEROS OF THE RIEMANN
ZETA FUNCTION IN SHORT INTERVALS
BIAO WANG
Abstract. Recently, on the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function, it is obtained
by Alpöge and Furman that more than 67.25% of the zeros are simple and on the critical
line, and more than 83.62% are distinct. Later, Lamzouri gave a different and more direct
proof. In this article, we will use the method of Lamzouri to give lower bounds on the
number of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function in short intervals. To prove the
main result, we establish Montgomery’s theorem on the pair correlation of zeros of the zeta
function in short intervals by following the approach of Baluyot, Goldston, Suriajaya and
Turnage-Butterbaugh, and then use Lamzouri’s inequality on any finite multiset of complex
numbers which is invariant under complex conjugation.
1. Introduction
Let ζ(s) be the Riemann zeta function. In 1859, Riemann conjectured that all the non-trivial
zeros of ζ(s) lie on the critical line ℜ(s) = 1/2. In 1914, Hardy [9] proved that infinitely many
zeros lie on the critical line, and in 1942 Selberg [20] proved that a positive proportion of
the zeros lie on the critical line. In 1947, Min [15] obtained an explicit lower bound for this
proportion. In 1974, Levinson [14] introduced a new mollifier method and proved that more
than one third lie on the line. In 1989, Conrey [5] raised this proportion to more than two
fifths. Subsequent developments of the mollifier method include the work of [8, 22, 19, 6].
Besides the mollifier method, a complementary approach uses pair correlation and inequalities
for finite collections of zeros. In 1973, Montgomery [16] initiated this approach and proved,
under the Riemann hypothesis, that at least two thirds of the zeros are simple. Montgomery
and Taylor [17] subsequently obtained the following larger proportion
