On the OpenAI proof that
Landau–Siegel zeros do not exist
WILLIAM D. BANKS
Abstract. A recent manuscript of OpenAI asserts that Landau–Siegel zeros do not
exist. Here, we give a detailed account of its argument, arranged as a direct proof with
explicit constants. There are absolute constants q0 and δ0 > 0 such that, for every
primitive real Dirichlet character χ of conductor q ⩾q0, every real zero β ∈(0, 1) of
L(s, χ) satisfies (1 −β) log q ⩾δ0, and one can take
q0 = 1010
and
δ0 = 3.0 × 10−3.
Combined with a recent computation of Lu, Zaman, and Zhao, this result yields the
bound (1−β) log q ⩾3.0×10−3 for every conductor q ⩾3. The proof is an interpolation
determinant argument in the biquadratic field generated by
√
2 over the quadratic field
attached to χ. A real zero close to one forces χ(p) = −1 for most primes p, and the
Frobenius congruence at these primes makes a certain nonzero determinant divisible by
higher powers of these primes than its Archimedean size allows. The underlying ideas
are those of the OpenAI manuscript. The explicit constants, and the presentation, are
new.

1. Introduction and statement of results
1.1. Background. Let χ be a primitive real Dirichlet character of conductor q. For a
suitable absolute constant c1 > 0, the function L(s, χ) has at most one zero β in the
interval 1 −c1/ log q ⩽β < 1, and such a zero is simple; see [1, Ch. 14]. A zero of
this kind is called a Landau–Siegel zero. Whether such zeros exist is one of the central
problems of analytic number theory, since they obstruct uniform estimates for primes in
arithmetic progressions and effective lower bounds for class numbers of quadratic fields.
Siegel [9] proved that L(1, χ) ≫ε q−ε for every ε > 0, and hence 1 −β ≫ε q−ε, but
his method gives no means of computing the implied constant. Page [7] showed that, for
every Q ⩾3, at most one primitive real character of conductor at most Q has a zero in
the interval (1−c2/ log Q, 1), where c2 > 0 is an absolute constant. On the effective side,
the work of Goldfeld [2] and of Gross and Zagier [3] yields lower bounds of logarithmic
order for the class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields, which is far from what the
nonexistence of Landau–Siegel zeros would give.
The manuscript [6] of OpenAI, dated October 1, 2026, asserts that Landau–Siegel
zeros do not exist. More precisely, it states that there is an absolute constant c > 0
such that (1 −β) log q ⩾c for every real zero β ∈(0, 1) of L(s, χ). The proof given
there is quite short. It is an interpolation determinant argument of a kind familiar from
transcendence theory [4], and its only analytic input is a classical inequality for the
logarithmic derivative of L(s, χ).
The argument of the present paper is the argument of [6], and we claim no originality
for its ideas. Our aim is to make that argument more accessible and to confirm it. The
proof in [6] is terse, and it proceeds by contradiction along a sequence of characters, so
it provides no numerical value for any constant. Here the proof is arranged as a direct
argument in which every step is written out, and the notation has been chosen with the
reader in mind. The explicit constants q0 and δ0 of Theorem 1.1 are new.
The paper was prepared as follows. The author first studied the manuscript [6], and,
with the assistance of the large language model Claude (Anthropic), produced an initial
report on the manuscript and an expanded draft of the proof. That draft is the basis of
the present text. The author worked through the argument line by line, rewriting proofs
and changing notation to aid understanding. Every line of the paper has been verified
by the author, who believes the argument to be sound and takes full responsibility for
the present text. In view of the importance of the statement, independent scrutiny is
essential; this paper is written to make that scrutiny as easy as possible.
1.2. Results. In this paper we give a detailed proof that Landau–Siegel zeros do not
exist, in a form that is explicit for all large conductors. The statement is that of [6]
(where no constant is given a numerical value) together with admissible values of the two
constants that govern large conductors. Our main result is the following.
Theorem 1.1. There are absolute constants q0 ⩾9 and δ0 > 0 such that for every
primitive real Dirichlet character χ of conductor q ⩾q0, every real zero β ∈(0, 1) of
L(s, χ) satisfies
(1 −β) log q ⩾δ0.
One can take q0 = 1010 and δ0 = 3.0 × 10−3. Consequently, there is an absolute constant
c > 0 such that (1 −β) log q ⩾c for every real zero β ∈(0, 1) of L(s, χ), for every
primitive real Dirichlet character χ of conductor q ⩾3. By the computation of [5], one
can take c = 3.0 × 10−3.

Proof of the second assertion from the first. There are only finitely many primitive real
characters χ of conductor q < q0. For each of them, the entire function L(s, χ) is not
identically zero, so it has only finitely many zeros in [0, 1]. Hence (1 −β) log q takes only
finitely many values, all of them positive, as χ runs over these characters and β over the
real zeros of L(s, χ) in (0, 1). Let c0 be the least of these values, or c0 := δ0 if there are
none. Then the second assertion holds with c := min{δ0, c0}.
For the last assertion, we take q0 = 1010 and δ0 = 3.0 × 10−3. By [5, Theorem 1.1],
which rests on a computation of Lu, Zaman, and Zhao, the function L(s, χ) has no real
zero β ⩾1 −1/(5 log q) when q ⩽1010. Hence c0 > 1
5 > δ0, and therefore c = δ0.
